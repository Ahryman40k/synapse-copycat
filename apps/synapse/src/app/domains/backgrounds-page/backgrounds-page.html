Background manager

A folder of images, the colours in each of them, and a group to give those colours to. Setting the wallpaper and setting the lighting become one gesture, so the desk and the display agree.

What it will do

  1. Keep a library of images from a folder you point at.
  2. Pull a handful of colours out of each — not one average, which on most photographs is mud.
  3. Apply one to a group: the wallpaper is set and the lighting takes a colour from the same picture.

Only the last step touches anything that exists today — a group's ambience already takes a fixed colour, and handing it one is a call that is written.

The hard part

Setting a wallpaper on Linux means driving whatever your desktop uses, and there is no common way in. This will be a set of adapters, not a call:

GNOME
GSettings — and the dark variant separately, or switching theme brings the old one back.
KDE Plasma
No setting to write: it takes a Plasma script, evaluated by the shell over DBus.
XFCE
xfconf-query, once per monitor and per workspace — the property path contains both.
swww, hyprpaper
Their own client or their own socket, and each one different.

Whichever of those land will say which ones were found on your machine, rather than offering one control that silently does nothing on three desktops out of four.
