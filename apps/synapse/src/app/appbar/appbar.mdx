## Depth

<Canvas of={stories.AgainstThePageBar} />

| Bar             | Surface role        | Tone |
| --------------- | ------------------- | ---- |
| Application bar | `surface`           | 0.14 |
| Page bar        | `surface-container` | 0.20 |

The application bar is **always the darker of the two**, for any colour a device
reports — lightness comes from the tone ladder, never from the source colour, so
the ordering cannot invert. That is what the two surface roles are for; swapping
them would flatten the hierarchy.

## Where you are

<Canvas of={stories.CurrentEntry} />

The bar had no notion of the current page: every entry looked the same wherever
you were. The current one now carries `aria-current="page"` — what a screen
reader announces, and what the theme file selects on.

Only the label is recoloured, where the page bar uses a **filled pill** — the
two levels of navigation stay told apart while sharing the same colour.

Exactly one entry is current at a time; on the dashboard, that is Home.

> The current entry is signalled by colour alone (WCAG 1.4.1). `aria-current`
> covers assistive technology, but `primary` and `on-surface-variant` sit at
> tones 0.72 and 0.76 — close enough in lightness that colour vision deficiency
> may flatten them. A background tint would fix it without a border.

## Nothing data-driven is in here

The bar renders a fixed table and nothing else. It got there in two steps, and
both are worth keeping because both were mistakes made first.

### Devices went first

They were one entry per kind — `mouse (1)`, `mouse (2)` — and both halves of
that were wrong.

The labels came from the kind because device names are long and inconsistent
and a bar has to fit a window someone can make narrow. But `mouse (2)` is not
something a reader can match to the thing on their desk, which is the one job a
navigation label has. And the entry led to a per-device page that competed with
the dashboard for the same work, while every control on it wrote to local state
and reached no hardware at all.

A device is opened from its own tile now — with its picture and its real name,
inside the group driving it — and what opens is a dialog, not a page. Those
routes are gone.

### Modules went with the command behind them

They were the last data-driven entries, and they were rendered from a `modules`
command **Rust never registered**. Under Tauri the call rejected; the browser
mock answered happily, which is what kept it invisible. Clicking one then threw
`NG04002: Cannot match any routes` — the bar offered a destination
`app.routes.ts` had never had a route for, and the guard meant to catch that
could not fire, because an unmatched segment rejects rather than resolving
`false`.

The whole feature was removed rather than routed: nothing on the Rust side
emitted a module, so there was no shape to build a page against.

### What went with them, and why it is written down

The module entries were the only ones that could overflow, so the **`⋯` menu
went too** — along with its `IntersectionObserver`, its stories and its tests.
The fixed places never overflowed: they carried no observed ref, so no
reachable behaviour was lost.

It is recorded here rather than only in git because the design took two attempts
and the second one is not obvious:

- Entries that did not fit moved into a menu; they did **not** scroll out of
  sight. With the scrollbar hidden the overflow was invisible, and with it shown
  it would eat a third of a 2.5em bar. Neither is reachable with a mouse without
  hunting.
- Only the **entries region** was clipped, never the host. An `overflow: hidden`
  ancestor clips absolutely positioned descendants as well, so a menu rendered
  inside the clipping box is in the DOM and invisible — exactly what happened on
  the first attempt.
- Which entries fit was decided by an `IntersectionObserver`, not by width
  arithmetic. Clipped buttons kept their box — `[hidden]` plus
  `visibility: hidden` rather than `display: none` — so they stayed in the
  layout. That is what stops the classic oscillation: remove an item, it frees
  room, which makes it fit, which puts it back. `[hidden]` still took it out of
  the tab order and the accessibility tree, so it was reachable in exactly one
  place: the menu.
- The observer's root was the entries region, so no `rootMargin` fudge was
  needed — the trigger sat beside the clipped box rather than over it.

`.bar__entries` still clips, and is still the right boundary. The day an entry
comes back that is rendered from data, lift that machinery out of history rather
than rediscovering it.

## It stays dumb

The bar emits which place was asked for; `DefaultLayout`, which already owns the
`Router`, navigates and feeds `place` back. `Navigation.location` reads it from
the **URL**, not from what was last clicked, so it survives a back/forward and a
page opened directly on its address. The bar never imports the router.

## Theming

| Part           | Role                                 |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| bar            | `surface`                            |
| entry          | `on-surface-variant`                 |
| entry, hover   | `on-surface` on a 10% `primary` tint |
| entry, current | `primary` label                      |
| focus ring     | `primary`                            |

Entries recede through a **text role, not opacity** — dimming a label lowers its
contrast, the opposite of "less prominent but readable". Both pairs are asserted
above 4.5:1 for every source colour in
`libs/ui/src/lib/theming/palette.spec.ts`.

The previous file used `color.invert($background-color)` for the label and
hardcoded `white` and `#111111` for the hover, none of which followed the theme.
